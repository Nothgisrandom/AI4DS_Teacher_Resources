# Checking AI statistical claims

A 45-minute lesson for introductory statistics or data science classes.

## Learning objectives

Students will calculate and interpret the mean and median, explain how an unusually large observation affects a summary, and distinguish evidence-supported statements from unsupported AI-generated claims.

## Preparation

- Students should know how to calculate a mean and median.
- Provide the [student activity](../05_Classroom_Activities/Checking_AI_Statistical_Claims_Activity.md).
- Use paper, calculators, or a spreadsheet. An AI account is optional; the activity includes an intentionally flawed example response.
- The dataset is synthetic. Do not substitute identifiable student records or send private classroom data to an AI service.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Opening discussion — 5 minutes.** Ask students how they decide whether a confident statistical explanation is trustworthy.
2. **Independent calculations — 10 minutes.** Students calculate the mean and median before reading the example AI response. Ask them to label units and explain what each number represents.
3. **Claim audit — 10 minutes.** Students examine each sentence of the example response, classify its claims, and identify what calculations or evidence would be needed to verify them.
4. **Sensitivity check — 10 minutes.** Students temporarily set aside the 45-minute observation and recompute the summaries. Discuss why this comparison does not justify deleting the observation from an analysis.
5. **Rewrite and reflection — 10 minutes.** Students write a corrected explanation and complete the exit ticket. Use the [rubric](../06_Assessments_and_Rubrics/Checking_AI_Statistical_Claims_Rubric.md) to assess their work.

## Teacher answer guide

The 10 completion times total 176 minutes. Their mean is **17.6 minutes** and their median is **14.5 minutes**, the average of 14 and 15. The range is **35 minutes**.

Temporarily excluding 45 gives a total of 131 minutes across nine observations: a mean of **14.556 minutes**, rounded to three decimals, and a median of **14 minutes**. The larger observation raises the mean substantially while affecting the median less. It may be a valid observation; investigate its context before deciding how to handle it.

The sample response gives the correct mean but an incorrect median. Its statement that all times are similar overlooks the 45-minute observation. No variable identifies AI use, and there is no comparison group or experimental design, so the dataset cannot establish that AI caused faster completion. These 10 synthetic observations also do not establish a typical completion time for a broader population.

One defensible rewrite is: “In this synthetic sample of 10 attempts, the mean completion time is 17.6 minutes and the median is 14.5 minutes. The 45-minute attempt pulls the mean upward. The data alone do not show whether AI affected completion time.”

## Adaptations

Provide the ordered values and a calculation scaffold for beginners. For advanced students, ask them to create a dot plot or investigate how different outlier definitions behave. Supply accessible plain-text data, and allow written, spoken, or spreadsheet-supported explanations.

## Optional AI extension

After the independent work, students may ask an approved AI tool to summarize the same synthetic data. Record the tool, date, and prompt, then verify every numerical and causal claim. The output may be correct or incorrect; evaluate the actual response rather than assuming an error will occur.
